Home Addition Electrical — Local Emergency Electrician

A new room, an ADU or a garage conversion adds real load to your electrical system, and the existing wiring was not planned for it. We wire home additions with new circuits and subpanels sized for the added space, so the new rooms have the power they need without overloading the rest of the house. Call (844) 957-4676.

Adding space to a home means adding circuits: lighting, outlets, heating or cooling, and often a kitchen or bathroom. The question is always whether the existing panel can carry the extra load, and whether the new space needs its own subpanel.

We plan addition wiring from the start, working with your builder or contractor. We check panel capacity, run the new circuits and subpanels, and wire the new rooms to the same standard as the rest of the home, with permits and inspections handled along the way.

What’s included

Our home addition electrical work includes:

  • Load calculation for the new space and the existing home
  • New circuits for lighting, outlets, heating and appliances in the addition
  • Subpanel installation when the addition needs its own panel
  • Panel upgrades where the existing service cannot carry the added load
  • Wiring for ADUs, in-law suites and detached additions
  • Garage conversion wiring, including outlets, lighting and heating circuits
  • Kitchen and bathroom wiring for additions, including GFCI protection
  • Permit handling and inspection support

Signs you need home addition electrical

You need addition electrical work if you are:

  • Adding a bedroom, living room, office or sunroom to your home
  • Building an ADU, in-law suite or guest house
  • Converting a garage, basement or attic into living space
  • Extending a kitchen or adding a bathroom
  • Unsure whether your panel can handle the extra load
  • Working with a contractor who needs a licensed electrician for the job

How it works

Addition wiring follows a clear process:

  1. 1. Plan and load check. We review the addition plans, calculate the new load and check whether your panel can carry it or needs a subpanel or upgrade.
  2. 2. Quote and permits. You get an upfront price covering the wiring, subpanel and any panel work, and we handle permits where required.
  3. 3. Rough-in. We run the new circuits and install the subpanel while the addition is framed, ready for inspection before drywall.
  4. 4. Trim-out and testing. After finishing, we fit outlets, switches and fixtures, test every circuit and support the final inspection.

Safety first

Emergency? If there is fire, smoke, or anyone is in danger, leave the building and call 911 (your local emergency number) first. Keep away from sparking equipment and never touch exposed wires.

An addition wired into an already-full panel is a common cause of overloaded circuits and nuisance tripping. We never add circuits to a panel that cannot safely carry them. If the load calculation shows the service is at its limit, we will explain whether a subpanel or a panel upgrade is the right answer.

Detached additions and ADUs need their own disconnect and proper grounding, and the feed between buildings must be buried or protected correctly. We install detached feeds to code so the new building is as safe as the main house.

Frequently asked questions

Will my panel handle a home addition?

It depends on your current service size and how much spare capacity you have. We do a load calculation for the existing home plus the addition and tell you honestly whether a subpanel, a panel upgrade or neither is needed.

What is the difference between a subpanel and a panel upgrade?

A subpanel adds extra breaker space near the addition and is fed from the main panel, which works when the main service has capacity. A panel upgrade increases the total service size and is needed when the existing service is already at its limit.

Can you wire a detached ADU or guest house?

Yes. We run a properly sized feed to the detached building, install its own panel and disconnect, and wire the interior to code, including grounding for the separate structure.

Do I need a permit for addition wiring?

Yes, in most areas. New circuits and panels for an addition need a permit and inspections at the rough-in and final stages. We handle the paperwork and arrange inspections.

Can you work with my contractor's schedule?

Yes. We coordinate rough-in and trim-out with your contractor's timeline so the electrical work does not hold up the build.
