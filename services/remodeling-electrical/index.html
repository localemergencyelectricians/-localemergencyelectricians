Remodeling Electrical — Local Emergency Electrician

A remodel is the best time to fix old wiring, add the circuits a modern kitchen or bathroom needs, and bring the room up to current code. We handle electrical work for kitchen, bathroom and basement remodels, including new circuits, GFCI protection, lighting plans and code upgrades. Call (844) 957-4676 to plan your remodel.

Remodels change how a room is used, and the wiring has to change with it. A new kitchen needs dedicated circuits for appliances, GFCI-protected outlets along the counters and lighting that actually works over the worktops. A new bathroom needs GFCI protection, a ventilation fan circuit and lighting that suits the space.

We work with homeowners and remodeling contractors to plan the electrical side of the job, from moving an outlet a few feet to rewiring the whole room. Because walls are open during a remodel, it is also the cheapest time to fix old wiring and bring the room up to code.

What’s included

Our remodeling electrical work includes:

  • Kitchen remodel wiring, including dedicated appliance circuits
  • Bathroom remodel wiring, including GFCI outlets and fan circuits
  • Basement finishing, including outlets, lighting and egress requirements
  • Lighting plans, including recessed, under-cabinet and task lighting
  • Outlet relocation and additions for new layouts
  • GFCI and AFCI protection upgrades to current code
  • Old wiring replacement while walls are open
  • Coordination with your contractor's schedule and inspections

Signs you need remodeling electrical

Your remodel needs electrical work if:

  • You are remodelling a kitchen, bathroom or basement
  • The room's outlets are in the wrong places for the new layout
  • New appliances need dedicated circuits the room does not have
  • The kitchen or bathroom has no GFCI protection
  • The room still has old wiring, two-prong outlets or a single circuit
  • You want new lighting, such as recessed lights or under-cabinet lighting

How it works

Remodel electrical work follows your contractor's schedule:

  1. 1. Plan the layout. We review the remodel plans with you and your contractor, confirm outlet and lighting locations, and check the panel capacity.
  2. 2. Quote and permits. You get an upfront price, and we handle permits and schedule inspections where required.
  3. 3. Rough-in. While walls are open, we run new circuits, move or add boxes, and replace any old wiring in the room.
  4. 4. Trim-out and testing. After finishing, we fit outlets, switches and fixtures, test every circuit and support the final inspection.

Safety first

Emergency? If there is fire, smoke, or anyone is in danger, leave the building and call 911 (your local emergency number) first. Keep away from sparking equipment and never touch exposed wires.

Kitchens and bathrooms are wet areas, so GFCI protection is essential and required by code. We fit GFCI outlets in every location that needs them, and we never reuse old wiring that shows damage, brittle insulation or signs of overheating.

A remodel is also the right time to check what is behind the walls. We inspect the existing wiring while it is exposed and will tell you honestly if anything needs replacing before it is covered up again.

Frequently asked questions

How many circuits does a new kitchen need?

A modern kitchen usually needs several dedicated circuits: two for countertop outlets, plus separate circuits for the fridge, dishwasher, microwave and oven. We plan the circuits from your appliance list and the layout.

Does a bathroom remodel need GFCI outlets?

Yes. Code requires GFCI protection for all bathroom outlets, and a bathroom usually needs its own 20A circuit. We also wire ventilation fans and heated floors where required.

Can you move outlets during a remodel?

Yes. Moving outlets and switches is straightforward while walls are open, and it is the right time to put them exactly where the new layout needs them.

Do you work with my remodeling contractor?

Yes. We coordinate with your contractor's schedule for rough-in, inspections and trim-out, so the electrical work fits the overall timeline.

Should I replace old wiring during a remodel?

If the walls are open and the wiring is old, damaged or ungrounded, yes. It is far cheaper to replace it during a remodel than to open the walls again later. We will show you what we find and advise honestly.
