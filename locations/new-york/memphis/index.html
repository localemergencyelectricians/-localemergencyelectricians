Common electrical problems in Memphis

These are the electrical problems we are called out for most often in Memphis. If anything below sounds familiar, call us before a small fault turns into a bigger one.

Outdated panel or two-prong outlets

Many older homes in Memphis still run on panels and wiring that were never designed for today’s loads — multiple computers, EV chargers, and electric appliances. Two-prong outlets, fuse boxes, and panels with no room for new circuits are all signs your system needs an upgrade for safety and capacity.

What to do: We can inspect your panel, explain your options, and upgrade you to a modern 200-amp service with room to grow. Call (844) 957-4676 to book an assessment.

EV charger or new appliance circuit

Level 2 EV chargers, induction cooktops, and heat-pump dryers all need a dedicated 240-volt circuit — and often a panel with spare capacity. In Memphis, we install the circuit, the charger, and any panel work needed, and handle the permit paperwork where required.

What to do: Tell us the charger or appliance model when you call, and we will confirm what your panel can support and give you upfront pricing.

Breaker keeps tripping

A breaker that trips again and again usually means an overloaded circuit, a short circuit, or a ground fault — not just a faulty breaker. In Memphis homes, the usual culprits are space heaters, window air conditioners, or too many high-draw appliances sharing one circuit. Repeated tripping is your electrical system asking for help, and ignoring it can damage wiring over time.

What to do: Unplug what you can from the affected circuit, reset the breaker once, and if it trips again, leave it off and call us. We will find the cause and fix it properly.

What to do now

  1. Stay safe first. If there is fire, smoke, or anyone is in danger, leave the building and call 911 (your local emergency number) first. Keep away from sparking equipment and never touch exposed wires.
  2. If it is safe, switch off power to the affected circuit at your breaker panel and unplug appliances on that circuit.
  3. Make a note of what happened: which rooms lost power, which breaker tripped, and whether you noticed a smell, buzzing, or warmth at an outlet.
