Electrical Panel Upgrades — Local Emergency Electrician

If your breakers trip constantly, your panel is decades old, or you are adding an EV charger or air conditioning, your panel may be at its limit. An upgrade replaces it with 100A, 200A or larger service, giving your home safer power and room to grow. Call (844) 957-4676.

Your panel is the heart of your home's wiring, distributing power safely through breakers to every circuit. When a panel is too small, too old or damaged, the warning signs show up as tripping breakers, flickering lights and warm breakers.

An upgrade swaps the old panel for a modern breaker panel with the capacity your home needs today, plus room for an EV charger, a heat pump or an addition. Most upgrades are done in a single day, and we handle the utility coordination, permits and inspections.

What’s included

A panel upgrade from Local Emergency Electrician typically includes:

  • Load calculation to size your new service correctly (100A, 200A or larger)
  • Removal of the old panel and installation of a modern breaker panel
  • Service upgrade, including the service mast, meter base and grounding where needed
  • New breakers, including AFCI and GFCI protection where code requires it
  • Subpanel installation when extra circuits are needed away from the main panel
  • Grounding and bonding brought up to current code
  • Clear circuit labelling so you can find every breaker at a glance
  • Permit handling and inspection support, plus coordination with your utility

Signs you need panel upgrades

Consider a panel upgrade if you recognise any of these:

  • Breakers trip often, especially when several appliances run at once
  • Your panel is more than 25 years old, or still uses fuses instead of breakers
  • The panel feels warm, buzzes, or shows rust, scorch marks or a burning smell
  • You are adding an EV charger, air conditioning or a home addition
  • There are no spare breaker slots left for new circuits
  • Lights dim or flicker when the washer, dryer or air conditioning starts

How it works

A panel upgrade is a bigger job than a repair, so we plan it carefully:

  1. 1. Assessment and load calculation. We inspect your panel and service, work out your home's current and future load, and recommend the right service size.
  2. 2. Quote and scheduling. You get an upfront price and a date. We arrange the utility shut-off and any permits so the day runs smoothly.
  3. 3. Upgrade day. Power is off for part of the day while we swap the panel, reconnect every circuit and bring grounding up to code.
  4. 4. Inspection and testing. We test every circuit, label the panel, and arrange the final inspection where one is required.

Safety first

Emergency? If there is fire, smoke, or anyone is in danger, leave the building and call 911 (your local emergency number) first. Keep away from sparking equipment and never touch exposed wires.

Panel work is never a DIY job. Even with the main breaker off, the service lines feeding the panel stay live. Our electricians work with the utility to de-energise the service and verify every connection before power is restored.

If your panel is sparking, smoking or hot to the touch, do not open it or touch it. Leave the area and call us, or call 911 if there is fire or smoke.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a 100A or 200A panel?

It depends on your home's size and load. A 200A service suits most modern homes, especially with electric cooking, air conditioning or an EV charger. We do a load calculation and recommend the right size.

How long does a panel upgrade take?

Most upgrades are completed in one day. Power is usually off for several hours while the panel is swapped, so we plan the timing with you and aim to restore power the same day.

Will I need a permit for a panel upgrade?

In most areas, yes. Panel and service upgrades usually need a permit and an inspection. We handle the paperwork and arrange the inspection where required.

Can a panel upgrade fix my tripping breakers?

If an overloaded or failing panel causes the trips, yes. If a single circuit is the problem, the fix may be simpler, such as a new breaker or an extra circuit. We diagnose first and only recommend an upgrade when it is genuinely needed.

Do you also install subpanels?

Yes. A subpanel is a good option when you need extra circuits in a garage, workshop, addition or basement and your main panel is full or far away. We will advise whether a subpanel or a full upgrade is the better fit.
