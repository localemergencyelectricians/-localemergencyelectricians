Generator Services — Local Emergency Electrician

When the power goes out, a standby generator starts automatically and keeps your lights, fridge, heating and essentials running. We install standby and whole-home generators, fit transfer switches and handle the wiring, maintenance and repair, so an outage is an inconvenience instead of a crisis. Power outages are becoming more common, and a few hours without electricity can mean spoiled food, a flooded basement or a cold house. A standby generator solves that by switching on automatically within seconds of an outage and powering the circuits you choose until utility power returns.

We help you size the generator for your home, install the transfer switch that connects it safely to your panel, and keep it maintained so it starts when you need it. We also repair existing generators and install transfer switches for portable generators.

What’s included

Our generator services cover the full life of your backup power system:

  • Standby and whole-home generator installation, sized to your home's load
  • Automatic transfer switch installation and wiring
  • Manual transfer switches and interlock kits for portable generators
  • Load calculation to decide which circuits the generator should power
  • Generator wiring, grounding and code-compliant connections
  • Routine maintenance, including exercise runs and battery checks
  • Generator repair and troubleshooting for units that will not start or run
  • Permit handling and inspection support where required

Signs you need generator services

Consider a generator if any of these apply to you:

  • Your area loses power several times a year, or outages last for hours
  • You rely on powered medical equipment, or someone in the home does
  • You work from home and cannot afford to lose power during the day
  • You have a sump pump, well pump or electric heating that must keep running
  • You have lost food, or dealt with burst pipes, during a past outage
  • Your existing generator will not start, or has not been serviced in years

How it works

Getting backup power installed follows four steps:

  1. 1. Load assessment. We work out which circuits matter most to you, such as heating, refrigeration, lighting and medical equipment, and size a generator to match.
  2. 2. Quote and planning. You get an upfront price covering the generator, transfer switch, wiring and permits, plus a clear installation date.
  3. 3. Installation. We install the generator and transfer switch, wire it into your panel and test the automatic switchover.
  4. 4. Handover and maintenance. We show you how the system works, set the exercise schedule and arrange routine maintenance so it stays ready.

Safety first

Emergency? If there is fire, smoke, or anyone is in danger, leave the building and call 911 (your local emergency number) first. Keep away from sparking equipment and never touch exposed wires.

A generator must never be connected to your home without a proper transfer switch or interlock. Backfeeding power into the grid through a regular outlet can electrocute utility workers and damage your home's wiring. We install the correct switching equipment for every generator, including portable units.

Generators produce carbon monoxide, so they must always run outdoors, well away from windows and doors. Never run a portable generator in a garage, basement or enclosed space, even with the door open.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a standby and a portable generator?

A standby generator is permanently installed, starts automatically when the power fails and powers selected circuits through a transfer switch. A portable generator is moved into place by hand, needs a manual transfer switch or extension cords, and must be refuelled regularly.

What size generator do I need?

It depends on what you want to power. A smaller unit covers essentials such as the fridge, lights and heating, while a larger whole-home unit can run air conditioning and everything else. We do a load calculation and recommend a size for your home.

Do I need a transfer switch?

Yes, for any generator connected to your home's wiring. A transfer switch isolates your home from the grid so the generator cannot backfeed power to utility lines, which is dangerous and illegal in most areas.

How often does a generator need maintenance?

Standby generators should be exercised regularly and serviced at least once a year, including oil, filters, battery and a full load test. We offer routine maintenance to keep your unit ready.

Can you repair my existing generator?

Yes. We troubleshoot and repair standby and portable generators, including units that will not start, run roughly or fail to transfer power. Call (844) 957-4676 to describe the problem.
