Need a new outlet where there is none, a ceiling fan installed, or a dedicated circuit for a new appliance? A licensed electrician can install it safely and to local code, usually in a single visit. Good electrical installation is invisible: outlets exactly where you need them, lights that work first time, and circuits sized for the load they carry. Poor installation shows up later as tripping breakers, warm outlets and failed inspections, which is why every install we do is wired to code and tested before we leave.

We handle the everyday installs that make a home work better, from extra outlets in a home office to ceiling fans, hardwired detectors and dedicated circuits for ovens, dryers and workshops.

What’s included

We install new electrical work for homes of every size, including:

  • New 120V and 240V circuits, including dedicated circuits for appliances
  • Outlets and switches, including USB outlets and dimmers
  • Light fixtures, chandeliers, pendants and under-cabinet lighting
  • Ceiling fans, including fan-rated boxes and bracing
  • Hardwired smoke and carbon monoxide detectors
  • Outdoor outlets, lighting and power for patios and gardens
  • Garage and workshop power, including subpanel feeds
  • Wiring for new appliances, hot tubs and home offices

Signs you need electrical installation

You probably need new electrical installation if any of these sound familiar:

  • You rely on extension cords or power strips because there are not enough outlets
  • A new appliance needs a dedicated circuit or a 240V outlet
  • A room has no ceiling light, or the only switch is in an awkward spot
  • Your smoke or CO detectors are battery-only, missing or out of date
  • You are setting up a home office, workshop or home gym that needs more power
  • An outdoor area has no power for lighting, tools or entertaining

How it works

A typical installation follows four simple steps:

  1. 1. Talk through the job. Call (844) 957-4676 and tell us what you need installed and where. We will ask about your panel and the age of the home so we arrive prepared.
  2. 2. On-site check and quote. The electrician confirms the route for the wiring, checks the panel has capacity, and gives you an upfront price before any work starts.
  3. 3. Installation. We run the wiring, fit the boxes, devices and fixtures, and connect everything to code, keeping disruption and dust to a minimum.
  4. 4. Test and walkthrough. Every new circuit is tested under load, and we walk you through what was installed and how to use it.

Safety first

Emergency? If there is fire, smoke, or anyone is in danger, leave the building and call 911 (your local emergency number) first. Keep away from sparking equipment and never touch exposed wires.

New wiring is only safe when it is sized correctly and connected properly. An undersized circuit overheats, a loose connection arcs, and an ungrounded outlet leaves you unprotected. Our electricians size every circuit for its load, use the right cable and breakers, and test grounding before the job is signed off.

If your home has old wiring, such as knob-and-tube or aluminium wiring, new installs need extra care and sometimes a different approach. We will tell you honestly if your existing wiring affects what can be installed safely.

Frequently asked questions

Can you install fixtures or fans I have already bought?

Yes. We are happy to install customer-supplied fixtures, fans and devices, and we will check they are complete and suitable for the location before fitting them.

Do I need a permit for a new circuit?

It depends on your local rules and the size of the job. A simple outlet swap usually does not, while new circuits and panel work often do. We will tell you what applies in your area and can handle the permit paperwork where needed.

How long does an installation take?

A single outlet or ceiling fan is usually done within an hour or two. Several new circuits or a full room of fixtures can take a full day. We will give you a time estimate with your quote.

What is a dedicated circuit, and do I need one?

A dedicated circuit serves one appliance, such as an oven, dryer, microwave or EV charger, so it cannot be overloaded by anything else. Most large appliances need one, and code requires it in many cases.

Can you add outlets without tearing up my walls?

Often, yes. We fish wires through walls, attics and basements to keep cutting to a minimum. Some jobs need small access holes, which we patch neatly.
